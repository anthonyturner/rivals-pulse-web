The short version

  • This website has no analytics, and you never need an account to read it. You can sign in with Discord if you want an account; see Accounts and signing in below. Reading the site sets no cookies of its own; only starting to sign in does (a short-lived rp_pkce cookie, then the rp_session sign-in cookie), and while you sign in your browser talks to Discord and to Supabase, our database service, directly. Like any website, its host may keep short-lived technical request logs.
  • Every page loads Google AdSense, so your browser also talks to Google; see Advertising below.
  • The community pages show anonymous totals only: no names, no player ids and no individual matches.
  • The totals come from matches uploaded by the RivalsPulse desktop app, a separate product that runs inside the Overwolf client.
  • The desktop app's own privacy policy is the full account of what the app collects and how to ask for deletion. This page describes the upload and the site, and does not replace it.

What the app uploads today

Every match the desktop app records is uploaded automatically: the app has no setting to turn it off, and the other players in the match are not asked. The app uploads the scoreboard line of every human player in it, on both teams, and a few facts about the match. The scoreboard figures are what the game itself shows.

The upload also carries a short-lived Overwolf sign-in token, which our server checks with Overwolf and does not store. We keep a one-way code made from the uploading player's Overwolf username, never the username itself, to limit how many matches one player can send. The same code is stored on every scoreboard line that player records.

For the uploading player we also keep a record of their own match history: the match facts and their own whole scoreboard line (player id, hero, team, kills, deaths, assists, final hits and result), under the name they play with. The same one-way code described above is also kept next to each match in that history, so that the history can be shown to the player once they link a website account. That history, and the code kept with it, has no time limit; it is capped per player (currently the most recent 5,000 matches).

Names and player ids are stored so that players can be looked up inside the desktop app. They are never published on this website.

Damage, healing, damage blocked and time per hero describe only the player who uploaded the match, the one person the app has those figures for. The app does not upload them for other players. The kill feed is reduced to hero against hero counts before it is sent, so it holds no player names or ids at all. These figures are used for community benchmarks, such as damage per minute, and for hero-against-hero counter numbers.

What this website publishes

  • Only anonymous aggregates: counts and sums over many matches, such as how often a hero is picked or wins, or the average match length on a map.
  • Never in-game names, player ids, or the rows of any single player or match.
  • A figure is shown only once it rests on a minimum number of matches. Below that the page says there is not enough data yet instead of showing a number.
  • A match recorded by two players is counted once, so one match cannot weigh twice.

How long it is kept

  • Scoreboard lines of all players, with names and ids, are deleted once the half-season after the one the match was played in has ended, and in any case no later than six months after the match. The figures being added above are kept for the same time.
  • The uploading player's own match history (their name, whole scoreboard line and the match facts), and the one-way recorder code kept next to each of its matches, is not deleted on that schedule. It has no time limit and is removed only when a player passes the cap of 5,000 matches and the oldest are pushed out.
  • Before a half-season's lines are deleted, its totals are saved as anonymous counts that hold no name, id or match. Those counts are what the community pages keep showing.
  • A player who does not use the app can ask for their scoreboard lines to be deleted and for their id not to be stored again. How to ask is in the desktop app's privacy policy. That deletion covers the scoreboard lines, not the uploading players' own match history above.

Accounts and signing in

Signing in is optional and works with Discord only. Nothing on the community pages needs an account.

  • What is stored. When you sign in, Supabase, the database service this website runs on, creates an account for you. It keeps your Discord user id, your Discord username, the email address on your Discord account if Discord shares one, the time you signed in, and the date the account was created. Discord also sends a few profile details with these, such as an avatar link; Supabase keeps them, and this website does not show or use them. We add one row of our own: your account id and the date it was created.
  • Your IP address. While you sign in, your browser talks to Supabase directly, not through this website. Supabase Auth records your IP address in its audit log when you sign in, and Supabase's request logs also see your IP address and browser details. We do not read or use them, and we have not set how long Supabase keeps them.
  • What it is used for. Signing you in and showing you your own account page: how you signed in, your email address and when you joined. We send no email, and there is no marketing.
  • Not connected to your matches. An account is not linked to your in-game name or to any match the desktop app uploaded, and the account page shows no game data. If that ever changes, this page will say so first.
  • The sign-in cookie. Signing in sets one cookie, rp_session. It keeps you signed in until you sign out, or until 30 days pass without a visit: each visit that renews your sign-in also renews the 30 days. It is hidden from page scripts and sent only to this site's own /api address. While you are in the middle of signing in, a second cookie, rp_pkce, lasts at most ten minutes and is removed when the sign-in finishes. Neither is used for tracking.
  • Who else sees it. Discord handles the sign-in and applies its own privacy policy to it, and Supabase stores the account. Your account details are not shown to other visitors and are not used in the community numbers.

Deleting your account. Sign in, open your account page, choose what to remove, type the confirmation phrase and press the delete button. Deleting needs a sign-in from the last ten minutes, so you may be asked to sign in again first. Removing the account deletes your sign-in details from our database service and our own row for you at once, and signs you out. Signing in with Discord again afterwards starts a new, empty account. It does not erase everything: Supabase keeps sign-in and request log entries for its own retention period, which we have not set, and those can include your account id, email address and IP address. Backups can also hold the account until they age out. Deleting the account here does not remove this site from your Discord settings; to do that, open Authorized Apps in your Discord settings and remove it there. A second choice, "Remove account and forget me", will also ask us to forget the matches the desktop app uploaded about you; it cannot be undone, and anonymous totals already published keep their counts. It is switched off until you link the desktop app to your account.

Advertising

This website uses Google AdSense, so every page loads Google's AdSense script. When it loads, your browser sends Google your IP address, browser details and the page you are on, and Google may set or read its own cookies or similar browser storage. Google handles that data under its own policy, how Google uses information from sites that use its services. We see only aggregate reports, such as ad views, clicks and earnings by page, country or device, never data about you.
