# RivalsPulse Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 11 November 2026**

RivalsPulse is an Overwolf app for the game Marvel Rivals. It shows you a
desktop window and in-game widgets built from what happens in your
matches.

This policy explains what RivalsPulse collects, who can see it, how long we keep
it, and how to have it deleted. It also covers players who never installed
RivalsPulse but appear in a match with someone who did.

It covers the RivalsPulse app only. Overwolf, the platform RivalsPulse runs on,
collects data under
[its own privacy policy](https://legal.overwolf.com/docs/overwolf/platform/platform-privacy-policy/),
and Marvel Rivals is run by NetEase under theirs. We are not a party to either.

**Who we are.** RivalsPulse is an independent app. It is not affiliated with,
endorsed by, or sponsored by NetEase, Marvel, or Overwolf. The developer of
RivalsPulse is responsible for the data described here (the "controller", in
data-protection law) and can be reached at the address in section 12.

## 1. The short version

- RivalsPulse learns about your matches **only from Overwolf's official game
  feed**. It does not look players up on any third-party statistics website.
- Your full match details stay **on your own computer**. The main things that
  leave it are listed in section 5: every player's scoreboard line, the banned
  heroes, the kill feed counted by hero, every player's hero picks and when they
  happened (no names), and your own damage, healing, damage
  blocked, time on each hero you played, how often you used each ability and
  ultimate, and the kills and assists soon after each use.
- **You choose whether matches are uploaded.** Settings has an **Upload my matches**
  switch, **on by default**. Turning it off stops future uploads, and a match you
  finish while it is off is never uploaded, even if you turn it back on
  (section 2.3).
- The **scoreboard line** of every player in a recorded match (name, player ID,
  team, hero, kills, deaths, assists, Final Hits, win or loss) is stored in our
  database, and **other RivalsPulse users can look it up**. That includes
  players who do not use RivalsPulse. During your own live match, from the start
  of hero select until it ends, the app does not look up another player or show
  their match history or profile, whether it comes from our database or from
  matches recorded on your PC. The best single-match kills, assists and
  Final Hits from those lines can also appear on the public **Pulse Records**
  page of our website: as "Anonymous" for a player with no linked website
  account, and with a name only for a player who chose to show it (section 4).
- Other players' **damage, healing, accuracy and damage blocked**, as seen in
  your matches, are never shown to you or stored. **Your own** figures on each
  hero are stored with your scoreboard line, and other users only ever see them
  **pooled** with everyone else's into averages per hero that name nobody
  (section 4). Those averages
  appear on our website and never in the game. The one exception is your choice:
  if you link a website account and switch on "Show me in Pulse Records", your
  own best damage, healing and damage blocked can appear on that page beside
  your name.
- RivalsPulse counts **Final Hits** (killing blows) for every player in your
  matches from the kill feed, the same as kills, deaths and assists. Those
  counts are part of each player's scoreboard line: kept with your matches on
  your own computer and stored in our database with the rest of the line.
- During a match, your in-game widgets show your **teammates' ultimate charge**, as the
  game does. For your opponents it shows **no ultimate information today**. The
  app counts how many times each opponent has used their ultimate this match, on
  your computer only, and does not show that count. A future version may show
  the count, and nothing more: it **never shows your opponents' ultimate
  charge** or when they last used it, because NetEase's rules forbid helping
  players predict opponents' ultimates.
- Anyone, including a player who does not use RivalsPulse, can **ask us to
  delete their data** (section 9).
- RivalsPulse shows **ads through Overwolf**. Overwolf and its partners collect
  data for that, and you can opt out.
- **Premium is optional.** If you buy it, Tebex and Overwolf take the payment.
  To check your plan, the app asks Overwolf, and starting Jarvis sends your
  Overwolf session to our server so it can do the same. We **do not store**
  anything about your subscription (section 2.6).
- We do **not** sell data, and we have no advertising business of our own.

## 2. What RivalsPulse collects

### 2.1 Your Overwolf account details

When the app starts, it asks Overwolf who you are. We use your Overwolf
username, display name and avatar to label your own data and show you whose
stats you are looking at. When the app sends a match to our database it also
sends a short-lived Overwolf sign-in token, which our server checks with
Overwolf to confirm the match came from a signed-in Overwolf user. The same kind
of token is used for Premium (section 2.6). We keep only
a one-way code made from your username, never the username itself. It is stored
on each scoreboard line you record (other users cannot see it) and used only to
limit how many matches one user can send.

The same sign-in token is how the app reads your match history back. Your
Match history screen asks our server for the matches your own sign-in uploaded,
and a player's page asks for that player's kills, deaths and assists per match.
The server answers only for a token it has confirmed with Overwolf. **Match
history is read only through your verified Overwolf sign-in**, never with the
app's public database key.

We never receive your Overwolf password. The app itself has no login. You can
choose to create an account on the RivalsPulse website and link it to the app
from Settings ("Link website account"). The link stores the in-game name and
player ID the app reports for you. Section 4 explains what the link means for
the website's Pulse Records page.

### 2.2 What happens in your matches

While Marvel Rivals is running, Overwolf's **Game Events Provider** (the official
Overwolf service that tells apps what is happening in a game) sends
RivalsPulse information about your match.

**About every player in the match, on both teams:**

- in-game name and player ID (the number the game uses to identify a player);
- team, chosen hero, and whether they are alive;
- kills, deaths and assists;
- Final Hits (killing blows), which RivalsPulse counts from the kill feed;
- rank, where the game provides it;
- the kill feed (who defeated whom) and the match result.

This is the same information the game shows on its own scoreboard, hero-select
screen and kill feed. Final Hits are counted on your computer, kept with the
match there, and sent to our database with each player's scoreboard line
(section 3). When the game hides a player's name, the kill feed cannot say whose
kill it was, so RivalsPulse leaves that player's Final Hits blank for the match
and sends no figure for them.

**About you only:** your damage dealt, damage blocked, damage taken, healing,
accuracy and ultimate charge. RivalsPulse also counts how often you use each
ability and your ultimate on each hero, from the cooldown timers and ultimate
charge the game feed reports, and the kills and assists in the seconds after each
use. Those counts show that you used an ability, not whether it hit anyone.

Your damage dealt, healing and damage blocked on each hero you played, how long
you played it, and those use counts are sent to our database with your scoreboard
line (sections 4 and 7). Your accuracy, damage taken, ultimate charge and match
totals are not.

**About your teammates, live only:** their ultimate charge. Your in-game widgets show
it during the match, as the game's own interface does. It is never stored in
our database or shared through lookups.

**About the match:** when it was played, how long it lasted, the map, the game
mode, which half-season it belongs to, and which heroes were banned.

**About heroes:** from the kill feed, how many times each hero defeated each
other hero in your matches. Only hero names and counts are kept, never player
names. RivalsPulse uses these counts on your computer to learn which heroes
counter which, and sends them to our database with each match, where they are
pooled into public hero statistics (section 4).

**About hero changes:** for every player in your matches, which heroes they
played, in order, and how many seconds into the match each pick happened. Only
the team, the heroes and the seconds are kept, never a name or player ID. They
are sent to our database with each match, where they are pooled into public
statistics on whether switching heroes helps a team win (section 4). The hero
picks are stored beside the scoreboard lines of the same match, so a pick list can
be matched to the line that ends on the same hero in the same team.

**About your opponents, live only:** RivalsPulse does **not** show any
ultimate information about your opponents today. During a match the app counts
how many times each opponent has used their ultimate, on your computer, from the
game events. It does not show that count today. The count is cleared when the
match ends, and is never stored in our database, shared through lookups or sent
to Jarvis. A future version may show the count, never charge or timing.

RivalsPulse **never shows an opponent's ultimate charge** or when they last used
their ultimate, and never tries to predict when an opponent will use it next.
NetEase's rules for Overwolf apps forbid helping players "predict opponents'
ultimate abilities". If Overwolf
sends any other detailed figure about another player, RivalsPulse does not show,
keep or send it.

### 2.3 Your settings

RivalsPulse stores your preferences on your computer: theme, which in-game
widgets are visible, their size and position, roster size, whether the Match
preview opens, and your recent player searches.

You can also save a **backup file** of your recorded matches and these
settings (Settings, "Back up and restore"). RivalsPulse writes it only where
you choose and never sends it anywhere. It holds the player names and IDs from
your scoreboards, so treat it as personal data (section 7).

**Upload my matches.** Settings has an **Upload my matches** switch under
**Privacy**. It is **on by default**, because the community statistics and your
match history on the website are built from uploaded matches. While it is on, each
match you finish is sent to our database, as sections 2.2 and 5 describe:

- every player's scoreboard line (name, player ID, team, hero, kills, deaths,
  assists, Final Hits and win or loss);
- every player's hero picks with their team and the second of each pick, with no
  names or player IDs;
- the kill feed counted by hero, with no player names, and the banned heroes;
- when the match was played, how long it lasted, the map, the game mode, the
  half-season and the match ID;
- your own damage, healing, damage blocked and time on each hero you played, and
  how often you used each ability and ultimate, with the kills and assists soon
  after each use.

Turning the switch off **stops future uploads**. Matches still save on your
computer, so your stats and history in the app keep working. A match you finish
while the switch is off is **never uploaded**, even if you turn it back on. A match
that was already waiting to upload when you turned it off is sent once you turn it
back on. Turning the switch off does **not** delete what was already uploaded;
section 9 says how to ask for that. Overwolf's own privacy settings (section 6)
are separate and cover Overwolf's data, not this upload.

### 2.4 The Jarvis voice assistant (off unless you turn it on)

RivalsPulse includes an optional voice assistant called Jarvis. It is **off by
default**. If you switch it on:

- while a conversation is open, your **microphone audio** is sent to
  **ElevenLabs**, the company that runs the voice and language service behind
  Jarvis;
- Jarvis can **start a conversation by itself**, for example to sum up a match
  when it ends, or to point out players you have met in earlier matches. That
  opens the microphone too, just as when you start one;
- so Jarvis can answer questions about your match, the app also sends it your
  own match figures and other players' **scoreboard lines only** (never their
  damage, healing, accuracy, damage blocked or ultimate charge);
- to stop the service being abused, our server records the **IP address** (the
  network address of your internet connection) that started each conversation.
  Records older than an hour are deleted the next time anyone starts a Jarvis
  conversation, so one can last longer than an hour if nobody does.

ElevenLabs handles that audio and text under
[its own privacy policy](https://elevenlabs.io/privacy-policy). Turning Jarvis
off stops all of this.

### 2.5 What we do **not** collect

- We do not collect your email address, real name, home address or payment
  details. If you buy Premium, Tebex and Overwolf take the payment (section
  2.6), not us.
- We do not read your keystrokes, your files, your browsing, or any program
  other than Marvel Rivals.
- We do not record your screen or your voice chat. The microphone is used only
  by Jarvis, only while you have it switched on and a conversation is open,
  including one Jarvis starts by itself.
- We have no analytics or tracking service of our own.

Premium is a paid plan. It removes the ads in the desktop app and lets you use
Jarvis. Everything else in RivalsPulse stays free. **Until Premium is switched
on in the app, nothing in this section happens.**

**Checking your plan on your computer.** When the desktop window starts, about
once an hour while it is open, and after you buy, RivalsPulse asks Overwolf's
subscription service whether you hold a Premium plan. It sends your short-lived
Overwolf sign-in token and the app's ID, and Overwolf answers with the state of
your plan. The app keeps that answer, which perks you have and until when, in

saves an answer for everyone, including players with no plan (an empty list of

after the last successful check, so a short outage does not cost you Premium.
The saved answer stays on your computer until the next check overwrites it, you
uninstall RivalsPulse, or you clear the app's cache in the Overwolf client
(section 7).

**Starting Jarvis.** While Premium is switched on, starting Jarvis sends your
Overwolf sign-in token to our server with the request for a voice conversation.
Our server checks the token with Overwolf, then asks Overwolf whether you hold
a plan, and allows or refuses the request. If Overwolf cannot be reached, Jarvis
does not start, even for subscribers. If you are signed out of Overwolf, Jarvis
cannot start either. The IP address record in section 2.4 is made before this
check, so it also covers a request that is refused.

**What we store about your subscription: nothing.** There is no subscriber list
or subscription table on our servers. Our server uses the token and the answer
to decide the request, and does not keep either. Like any server, it may keep
short-lived technical logs of a refusal (its status and reason, never your
token). We cannot see afterwards who subscribed.

**Payments.** Tebex and Overwolf take the payment and handle renewal,
cancellation and refunds, under their own privacy policies. We never see your
card or other payment details.

## 3. Players who do not use RivalsPulse

This has its own section, because it is the part people reasonably worry about.

When a RivalsPulse user plays a match, the app sees the other players in it
through Overwolf's game feed. **Those players have not installed RivalsPulse
and have not agreed to this policy.** Here is exactly what that means for them.

**What we store about you, and who can see it.** If you play in a match with a
RivalsPulse user, our database may hold your **scoreboard line** from that
match: your in-game name, player ID, team, hero, kills, deaths, assists, Final
Hits (killing blows, counted from the kill feed), and whether your team won.
Any RivalsPulse user can look up a player ID and see those lines for the
current half-season. During a user's own
live match, from the start of hero select until it ends, the app does not look
up another player or show their match history or profile. Other users cannot
see who recorded a match, and nobody can download the whole database.

**Pulse Records, a public page on our website.** If one of those lines holds one
of the highest single-match kills, assists or Final Hits, it can be shown on the
website's Pulse Records page, which anyone can view. The entry shows only the
hero, the figure and the word "Anonymous": no name, player ID, match, map or
date, and not who recorded the match. It is still a published line from a match
you played, and someone who played in it may recognise it. Section 4 says more,
and section 9 says how to have it removed.

**What we never store or share about you:** your damage, healing, accuracy,
damage blocked or ultimate charge. We never link your in-game name to a real
name, an email address or an account outside the game. The kill feed counts sent
with a match name heroes only. They are kept with the match until it is deleted,
and published only as totals per hero, never as a kill you made or suffered. The
same goes for which heroes you played and when: the match keeps each player's
heroes and the second of each pick with the team, and no name or player ID, and
they are published only as totals across many matches.

**Your ultimate charge.** If you are on a RivalsPulse user's team, your live
ultimate charge appears on their in-game widgets during the match, as it does in the
game's own interface. It is never stored or shared. If you are on the other
team, your charge is never shown and we never predict your ultimate, and their
in-game widgets show nothing about your ultimate today. Their app counts how many
times you have used it that match, on their computer only, without showing it;
that count is never stored or shared. A future version may show the count, never
your charge or timing.

**How long we keep it:** see section 7.

**How to have it deleted:** see section 9. You do not need to install
RivalsPulse to ask. Deletion covers our database. It cannot reach the copies
RivalsPulse users keep of their own matches on their own computers, which we
have no access to.

Because we have no way to contact players who do not use the app, this public
policy is how we tell you.

## 4. What other RivalsPulse users can see about you

If you use RivalsPulse, the same rule applies to you as to everyone else. Other
users can look up your **scoreboard lines** (name, player ID, team, hero, kills,
deaths, assists, Final Hits, win or loss) from matches recorded by any
RivalsPulse user, including you, for the current half-season. They cannot do this
during their own live match, from the start of hero select until it ends: for that
time the app does not look up another player or show their match history or profile. When they compare
heroes against other app users, they see kills, deaths, assists, win rate,
games played and heroes only.

Your own damage, healing, accuracy and damage blocked are shown as yours to
**you only**: in the app and, if you link a website account, your damage, healing
and damage blocked on your Profile page. Your damage dealt, healing, damage
blocked, time on each hero and ability and ultimate use counts are also stored in
our database with your scoreboard line, but other users never see them as yours:
only pooled, as below. The one exception is Pulse Records (below), and only if you
switch your name on there. Final Hits (killing blows, counted from the kill
feed) are counted for every player, the same as kills, deaths and assists, and
are part of the scoreboard line others can look up. Your live ultimate charge
is also shown to teammates who use RivalsPulse, during the match only, as the
game shows it; it is never stored or shared.

**Hero statistics anyone can see.** The heroes banned in each match you record
are sent to our database with that match (section 5). We pool them with the
matches every other RivalsPulse user records into **ban rates** (how often each
hero is banned) and publish those as public hero statistics, for example in the
RivalsPulse tier list. A ban rate is a count per hero. It never says who played
in a match, who recorded it, or which team banned the hero.

In the same way, your figures and use counts on each hero are pooled with every
other user's into **hero averages**, such as damage per minute, and scores worked
out from them, such as the build signals in the website's team builder. These are
published on the RivalsPulse website and never appear in the game. They never name
a player, show one player's numbers or say who recorded a match. A hero is shown
only once enough different players have contributed. Until then it says that data
is still being collected. Pulse Records, below, is the one page that does show one
player's numbers, and names only players who switched their name on.

The kill feed counts are pooled in the same way into **hero counters**: how often
each hero defeats each other hero. They never name a player or say who recorded a
match.

Hero changes (which heroes each player picked, and when) are pooled into
**counter-switch statistics**: how often a team wins after a player switched to a
hero while a given enemy hero was on the field. They name no player and say
nothing of who recorded the match.

**Pulse Records on our website.** Pulse Records is a public page on the RivalsPulse
website, which anyone can view. It lists the highest single-match figures from
Quick Match and Competitive matches recorded by RivalsPulse users, for kills,
assists, Final Hits, damage, healing and damage blocked. Each entry shows a hero, a
figure and either a name or the word "Anonymous". It never shows a player ID, the
match, the map, the date, or who recorded the match.

- **Your name appears only if you choose.** It is shown only if you link a website
  account to the app and switch on "Show me in Pulse Records" on the website's
  account page. It is off until you switch it on, and you can switch it off at any
  time. Your name is shown only on entries from matches your own app recorded. It
  is the in-game name stored with your link, which the app reported when you
  linked; we cannot check that it is yours.
- **Anonymous entries come from players who never agreed.** The kills, assists and
  Final Hits lists also include scoreboard lines of players who have no linked
  account, including players who never installed RivalsPulse. Such an entry shows
  the hero and the figure as "Anonymous", with no name or player ID, but it is
  still a published line from a match that player took part in, and someone who
  played in it may recognise it. A player whose account is linked is never shown
  as "Anonymous": if they have not switched their name on, their entries are left
  off, and so are lines of theirs that another user's app recorded.
- **Damage, healing and damage blocked** come only from the recording player's own
  line, because the app sends those figures for nobody else. Those lists show only
  players who switched their name on, with their own figures.
- **Taking an entry off.** Switching "Show me in Pulse Records" off removes your
  name and your entries within a few minutes. Unlinking or deleting your website
  account removes your name, but a kills, assists or Final Hits line of yours can
  then show as "Anonymous" until it is deleted on the schedule in section 7. The
  "forget me" choice on the website's account page, or a deletion request
  (section 9), deletes the lines themselves and takes the entries off.
- **Not proof.** Entries come from what the app reports, and a modified app can
  report false figures or a name that is not its player's.

## 5. Who we send data to

RivalsPulse is a desktop app, so most of its work happens on your computer. When
it does reach out, these are the only places it goes.

| Service | What is sent | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Supabase** (our database host) | Scoreboard lines of the players in your matches (name, player ID, team, hero, kills, deaths, assists and Final Hits), match details, banned heroes, the kill feed counted by hero (no player names), every player's heroes in order with the second of each pick (a team, hero IDs and seconds, no player names or IDs), and your own damage dealt, healing, damage blocked, time, and ability and ultimate use counts (with the kills and assists soon after each use) on each hero, plus your Overwolf sign-in token (checked, not stored) and a one-way code made from your Overwolf username, stored on each line you record | To store matches and let users look players up, to work out the public ban rates and hero averages, and to build the public Pulse Records page on our website from the stored lines (section 4) |

Like any web server, each of these sees the IP address your request comes from,
and may keep it in short-lived technical logs under its own policy.

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it with data brokers.

## 6. Advertising

RivalsPulse shows ads on pages of the desktop app, in a panel on the left,
through **Overwolf's advertising system**. It never shows them on the Settings
page.

It also shows one ad in the **Match preview**, a small window in the game that
shows the map and your own results on it before a match's first round. That window opens when a match
starts and closes when the first round starts, so **no ad is ever shown during
play, and none appears in any other in-game widget**. You can turn the Match
preview off in Settings, or close it for one match with its close button.

We do not run our own ad network and we do not choose which ads you see.
If you hold Premium, the desktop app shows no ads (section 2.6); the ad in the Match preview stays.

To serve those ads, Overwolf and its advertising partners collect data about
you, which may include your device and IP address, an advertising identifier,
and how you interact with the ads. This happens under
[Overwolf's privacy policy](https://legal.overwolf.com/docs/overwolf/platform/platform-privacy-policy/),
not this one. Overwolf takes part in the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework (an
industry system for recording ad consent) as consent manager number 246.

By installing RivalsPulse through Overwolf, you are also sharing your Overwolf
account email address, single-sign-on information and advertising-related data
with Overwolf, as Overwolf's developer terms require us to tell you.

**Your choices.** The Overwolf client's privacy settings let you opt out of
personalised ads. See
[Overwolf's data privacy options](https://support.overwolf.com/support/solutions/articles/9000189145-data-privacy-options).
They apply to Overwolf's advertising data, not to the matches RivalsPulse uploads
(section 2.3).

## 7. Where data is stored, and for how long

### On your computer

Your matches, including your own detailed figures and the scoreboard lines you
saw, your settings and your recent searches are stored in the app's local
storage inside the Overwolf client. They stay until you delete them.
Uninstalling RivalsPulse, or clearing the app's cache in the Overwolf client,
removes them, but **not a backup file** you saved (see below). This includes the saved answer to "do I have Premium?" described in
section 2.6, which the app keeps for every player until the next check
overwrites it or the storage is cleared; it stops counting about a day after
Overwolf last confirmed it.

### In a backup file you save

If you use "Back up" in Settings, RivalsPulse writes your recorded matches and
settings to a file in the folder you pick. That file holds the scoreboard lines
of those matches, including other players' names and IDs. It is **not** stored
inside the app, so uninstalling RivalsPulse or clearing its cache does **not**
remove it, and it stays until you delete it. We never receive it. The file
leaves your computer only if you move or share it yourself. It does not hold
your Premium status.

### In our database

Matches are stored in a **Supabase** database run for RivalsPulse, hosted in
**[SUPABASE REGION — to be confirmed]**.

- **Scoreboard lines and match details** are deleted at the end of the
  half-season after the one the match was played in, and never later than **six
  months** after the match. Your figures and use counts on each hero are stored
  with your line and deleted with it. A match's kill feed counts and hero changes
  are deleted on the same schedule.
- **Pulse Records entries** on our website are read from those scoreboard lines
  and the figures stored with them, and no separate copy is kept. An entry
  disappears when its line is deleted on the schedule above or on request
  (section 9), or when the player ID is on the do-not-store list. A name stops
  showing within a few minutes of its player switching "Show me in Pulse
  Records" off, unlinking, or deleting their website account (section 4).
- **Ban rates and hero averages** are kept as counts and averages per hero,
  pooled from every user's matches and published as public hero statistics
  (section 4). They hold no player names or IDs, so they are not personal data
  and may be kept longer.
- **Jarvis IP addresses** older than an hour are deleted the next time anyone
  starts a Jarvis conversation (section 2.4).
- **Subscriptions:** nothing about your subscription is stored in the database
  (section 2.6).

## 8. Legal basis (UK and EU users)

Where the UK GDPR or EU GDPR (data-protection law) applies, we rely on:

- **Legitimate interests** for reading match data and storing and sharing
  scoreboard lines. That interest is giving players match statistics, letting
  them look up people they have played with, and showing the best single-match
  kills, assists and Final Hits on the public Pulse Records page of our website,
  where a line appears as "Anonymous", without a name or player ID. The same
  limits apply to that public page as to lookups: we limit it to information every
  player in the match can already see in the game, on the scoreboard and in the
  kill feed, we never share anyone's detailed figures as theirs, we keep it for a
  short time, and anyone can object and have it deleted.
- **Legitimate interests** for storing your own figures and use counts on each

## 9. Your rights, and how to have data deleted

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask for a copy of the
personal data we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to
or restrict how it is used, and to complain to your data-protection regulator.

**If you use RivalsPulse,** you can delete everything on your own computer by
clearing the app's cache in the Overwolf client or uninstalling the app. Delete
any backup files you saved yourself; removing the app does not remove them. To stop
future uploads, turn off **Upload my matches** in Settings (section 2.3). For
data already in our database, contact us as below. Deleting your scoreboard lines also
deletes your figures and use counts stored with them. Hero averages not yet saved
for good are rebuilt without them within about an hour. Averages already saved for
older half-seasons name nobody, so they are not changed.

**Pulse Records.** Switching "Show me in Pulse Records" off on the website's
account page removes your name and your entries from the website's Pulse Records
page within a few minutes. Unlinking or deleting your website account removes
your name, but a kills, assists or Final Hits line of yours can then show as
"Anonymous" until it is deleted (section 7). To remove the lines themselves, use
the "forget me" choice on the account page, or ask us to delete your scoreboard
lines as below. The entries go with the lines.

**If you do not use RivalsPulse,** you can still ask us to delete your
scoreboard lines. Email us at the address in section 12 with:

- your **in-game name**, your **player ID**, or both;
- a screenshot of your in-game profile showing that name or ID, so we can tell
  the request comes from the player concerned.

We will delete the matching rows from our database and add your player ID to a
**do-not-store list**, so matches recorded later by other users do not store
your line again. Any Pulse Records entry made from those lines goes with them.
We will reply within 30 days.

This removes your data from **our database only**. RivalsPulse users keep the
scoreboards of matches they played on their own computers, including in any
backup files they saved (section 7). Those
copies are not on any server we control, so we cannot delete them.

Requests about advertising data, or about payments and your Premium plan, should
go to Overwolf and Tebex, who control them. Overwolf's address is
dpo@overwolf.com.
