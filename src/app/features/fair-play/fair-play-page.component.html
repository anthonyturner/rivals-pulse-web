The short version

  • The RivalsPulse app is not released yet. It is in development testing and is not on Overwolf. This page describes how the app is designed to work at launch.
  • RivalsPulse is built around the published policies for Marvel Rivals tools. It will not show you another player's damage or healing, it will never show an opponent's ultimate charge or timing, and it will never predict an opponent's ultimate.
  • It will store information about other players. Every match the app records will keep the scoreboard line of each human player in it, including players who never installed the app, and an anonymous record of the heroes each player switched to. Any RivalsPulse app user will be able to look a player up from the scoreboard lines.
  • There is no opt-out from being viewable by other RivalsPulse users yet; see Opting out.
  • This page is a plain summary. The privacy notes are the full account of what the app will upload, what is published and what is kept, and for how long.

What RivalsPulse does not show

  • Other players' damage, healing, accuracy or damage blocked. The app will never show you, store or share another player's damage, healing, accuracy or damage blocked. It keeps only your own.
  • An opponent's ultimate charge, or when they last used their ultimate. The most the overlay is allowed to show about an opponent's ultimate is a count of how many times they have used it in the match you are playing. Today it shows nothing about it. It will never show their charge or timing, and no feature of the app predicts when an opponent will use it.
  • Advice on which hero to ban. RivalsPulse's rule is that ban information is shown only as ban rates, how often each hero is banned across recorded matches, and that the app never suggests which hero to ban. This website shows no ban figures today.
  • Another player's scoreboard history or profile, during your own live match. At launch, during your own live match, the app doesn't look up or show another player's scoreboard history or profile.
  • One player's own figures, as theirs, apart from Pulse Records. The figures an app user records about their own play will be published on this website as averages per hero, never as one player's numbers and never with who contributed. The exception is Pulse Records, described below.

What it does collect and show

  • A scoreboard line for every human player in a match you record, on both teams. That is their in-game name, player id, team, hero, kills, deaths, assists, final hits, and whether their side won or lost. These lines will be stored on our server, and any RivalsPulse app user will be able to look a player up from them. That includes players who never installed the app.
  • An anonymous hero timeline for every player. For the players on each team, the app will upload the heroes they played in order and the second each pick happened, with no player id and no name. It will be stored under the same match as that match's scoreboard lines, so it can in practice be linked back to a scoreboard line: someone reading both can tell which player made which hero switches, and when.
  • The recording player's own figures. The player using the app will also upload their own damage, healing, damage blocked, time on each hero, and how often they used each ability and their ultimate, with the kills and assists soon after each use. These will be published on this website as averages per hero, apart from Pulse Records below, and a hero is held back until enough different players have contributed. While few players upload at first, an average can still mostly reflect one player's games.
  • Pulse Records, on this website. Pulse Records publishes the highest single-match figures, and it already does so today from matches played on development builds of the app. A player's own damage, healing and damage blocked appear beside their name only if they linked an account and switched their name on. Other players' kills, assists and final hits appear too, as "Anonymous" with no name or id, including players who never installed the app and never agreed to it.
  • Live, on your own overlay only. During a match the overlay will show your teammates' ultimate charge on your own screen. The most it is allowed to show about an opponent's ultimate is a count of their uses, which today it does not show. Neither is stored or sent anywhere.

At launch, uploads will be on by default. A player can turn them off in the app's Settings, under Privacy; while they are off, the app uploads nothing and matches still save on that player's own PC. The other players in a match will not be asked. How long lines are kept, and how a player who does not use the app can ask for theirs to be deleted, are in the privacy notes.

Opting out

An opt-out does not exist yet. A player cannot yet stop other RivalsPulse users from viewing their match history. One is planned, enforced on our server, and this page will change when it is built.

The policies behind this

Overwolf's page for Marvel Rivals has a Game compliance section (last checked on 10 November 2026). It says that NetEase's game compliance policy "prevents giving users confidential information like damage and healing statistics, selectively ban heroes, and predict opponents' ultimate abilities", and that Overwolf's own app policy forbids giving players any kind of advantage. The rules above are drawn from those policies.

  • That is Overwolf's summary of NetEase's policy, not NetEase's full text, and policies can change. If they do, this page will change with them.
  • Publishing per-hero averages from players' own figures is our reading of that summary, and we have not asked Overwolf or NetEase to confirm it. No lawyer has reviewed this page.
  • Policies can change, and what the game allows is NetEase's decision. The FAQ explains what our answer on bans rests on.
  • RivalsPulse is an independent project and is not affiliated with NetEase, Marvel or Overwolf.
