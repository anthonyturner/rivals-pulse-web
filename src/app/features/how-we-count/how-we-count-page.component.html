Where the numbers come from

Only from matches that RivalsPulse players record with the app and upload. There is no other source: no outside tier list, no other stats site, and no guesses to fill gaps. If not enough matches have been uploaded, the site says "not enough data yet" instead of showing a number. What the app uploads is in the privacy notes.

What an appearance is

An appearance is one hero on one scoreboard in one match. Everyone in the match is counted, on both teams. If the same hero is on both teams of one match, that match gives the hero two appearances, so a hero's appearances can be higher than the number of matches with a result, and a pick rate can go over 100%.

Win rate, pick rate, appearances and tiers use only matches that ended in a win or a loss. A match with no result is left out of them. KDA and final hits are different: they are averaged over every recorded line, including those from matches with no result.

Why a hero can be missing

A hero needs at least 10 appearances, recorded by at least 2 different players, before its win rate, pick rate and tier are shown. Below that, a few lucky or unlucky games, or one player's habits, would decide the number, so the site shows only how many appearances the hero has so far. A past half-season whose players were never counted needs the 10 appearances alone.

Ranks are blended

Every rank is counted together. A player's rank is not recorded with their matches, so there is no way to show one rank on its own.

Which game modes count

By default, Quick Match and Competitive are counted together. The tier list and the win rates page have a Mode picker that counts one mode on its own, and so does the switch on the Roles tab of the Stats page. The home page and the Stats tabs use the default, with these exceptions: the "Matches counted" figure on the home page and the Stats overview, the overview's "Community matches" mode mix, average match length and matches per day count every mode, and so does the mode table in My matches on the Profile page, which lists every mode you played. "Matches counted" is every match once, whatever its mode and whether or not it ended in a win or a loss. The win rates and tiers say "matches with a result" for the smaller count they are worked out from.

A mode is in the picker only once it has at least one recorded match with a win or a loss, so Arcade appears by itself when it has data. Arcade matches can have more than two teams and custom lobbies, so its win rates are less reliable and do not compare with the other modes. Practice is not offered yet: in Practice against bots the app currently records the bots as players, which would count them as people.

Competitive has far fewer recorded matches than Quick Match, so on its own most heroes fall below the minimum. When a mode has too little data in the current half-season, the page shows the previous half-season for that mode and says so.

A match where the game did not say which mode it was is left out of every win rate, pick rate and tier, because it could have been Practice or Arcade. The exceptions are "Matches counted" and that same mode table, which list it as Other.

Half-seasons

Numbers are counted one half-season at a time, such as Season 10 and Season 10.5. A new half-season starts with almost no matches. Until one hero in it has enough appearances and players, the site shows the previous half-season's numbers instead, as long as that one has some, and says so. The line under the home page buttons names the half-season its count is from.

Tiers and win rates

The win rate shown for a hero is its plain win rate. Its tier is placed from an adjusted win rate: the record, plus the average of the hero's role counted as if about 60 more matches had been played at that average. The size of that pull is worked out from how far apart the heroes' win rates really are, so it changes as matches are counted. A short winning streak cannot put a hero at the top, and even a hero with a long record sits a little closer to its role's average than its plain win rate. A hero's tier can therefore look different from its win rate alone.
