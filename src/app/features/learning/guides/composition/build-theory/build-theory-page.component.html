Composition

Utility builds and how they differ from damage, sustain, mobility, and ultimate plans.

A build is the fight pattern you choose to support: the tools you reserve, the ground you take and the opening your team intends to use. Marvel Rivals does not ask you to buy items mid-match.

Choose what the team will play around.

Start with the problem in the fight. If an enemy ability decides every engage, saving the tool that answers it may matter more than adding damage. If the team cannot finish a vulnerable target, the useful plan may instead be focused burst. The category describes how you intend to win the exchange, not a score assigned to your hero.

A utility plan asks which enemy option you can remove. Damage asks how to finish a target; sustain asks how to survive the response; mobility asks where the exchange should happen; an ultimate plan asks how to prepare the decisive cast. These aims help explain different priorities without requiring every fight to fit only one pattern.

Recognize a real utility plan.

Utility wins by changing choices. Work backward from the threat, decide which answer must remain available, then identify the event that makes it worth spending. An exit keeps that answer from becoming an unfavorable trade immediately afterward.

Worked example

Hypothetical: reserve the answer to a dive.

Your team is losing its supports when an enemy dives, even though you have a defensive tool that can help. A utility plan starts by keeping that answer for the dive rather than spending it during harmless pressure.

Call the trigger to your team, use the protection when the threat arrives, and keep an exit after the trade. If teammates cannot follow up or the ability does not answer the actual threat, reassess the plan instead of assuming that holding a cooldown is useful by itself.
