# Changelog

All notable changes to RivalsPulse are recorded in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).
How to add an entry: [docs/changelog.md](docs/changelog.md).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The Community Stats tab, Heroes tab, Counters tab and each hero page have new panels for damage, healing and damage blocked per minute, and for which heroes a hero is seen killing most and is seen killed by most in the kill feed, labelled as a separate measure from win rates. Each panel says "Not enough data yet" and names its minimum until enough different players have uploaded these figures, which is every panel today. Only rounded rates and volume bands leave the server, never a player's name, id or own row ([#178](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/issues/178)).
- The Privacy notes now say two things are planned: uploading how often you use each ability and your ultimate, and publishing hero averages from players' own figures, never showing one player's numbers ([#463](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/463)).
- The site now has a dark and a light theme that use the same colours as the RivalsPulse desktop app (its Aurora palette), and a button at the end of the header switches between them. The button says which theme it switches to, works with the keyboard, and sits in the menu on narrow screens. With no saved choice the site follows your device's light or dark setting (dark if it cannot tell); your choice is saved in this browser only, under `rp.web-theme`, and does not follow you to other devices or into your account. Every page was re-coloured for both themes: the gold accent is now the app's green, and the old fixed colours in page styles are replaced by shared theme colours. The page is set to the right theme before it is first drawn, so it does not flash the other one. A check of text and control contrast in both themes now runs with `npm run test:theme` ([#395](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/issues/395)).
- There is now a "My stats" page for signed-in players (link in the header menu, address `/profile`). It shows your Overwolf avatar and in-game name, your matches, win rate over the matches with a known result, kills, deaths and assists, final hits, your most played heroes, damage, healing and blocked per minute with how many matches they come from, your records and win streak, and your latest 10 matches. Something the desktop app did not record says "Not counted" instead of 0. If your account is not linked yet, the page explains how to link it; if the app has not uploaded a match yet, it says matches appear after it does. The page says history goes back about six months and that a match first uploaded by someone else in your lobby, or before 9 October 2026, may be missing. It shows no level, rank or Discord name ([#266](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/issues/266)).
- The site can now work out your own stats once your account is linked to your in-game player, ready for a dashboard page (no page shows them yet). It uses only the matches your own app uploaded under your own link: win rate, kills, deaths and assists, final hits, your most played heroes, damage, healing and blocked per minute, records and your latest matches. Only the results of those sums are sent to your browser, not the matches, and no one else can read them. A match the app did not record a figure for is left out of that figure instead of counting as 0. A match that someone else in your lobby uploaded first is not included, and history reaches back about six months at most ([#347](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/347)).
- Pulse Atlas, the public feed the Overwolf overlay reads, gains a `community-stats` file with how often each hero wins against, and together with, every other hero in the matches Rivals Pulse players upload (counts only, from Competitive and Quick Match, for one half-season). It appears after the database update and the next publish; until then, or if its source fails, the feed keeps the last published copy or leaves the file out, and nothing else is held up ([#360](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/360)).
- The server now records which verified app user uploaded each match in a new table that website visitors and the app cannot read (the public match table is unchanged), so a linked player's history can later be counted beyond six months. It starts empty and fills from the day it is deployed; uploads behave exactly as before, and it needs a database migration pushed by hand first ([#331](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/331)).
- You can link your account to your in-game player. In the RivalsPulse desktop app you ask for a one-time link code, then type it under "Link the desktop app" on your Account page. A code works once and for ten minutes, asking for a new one cancels the old, and a wrong, used or expired code gets one message ("That code is wrong or has expired. Get a new one from the desktop app."); five wrong codes in 15 minutes lock the page for a while. Once linked, the site header shows your in-game name and Overwolf avatar instead of "Account" (a long name is cut short; the screen-reader label still starts with "Account"), and your Account page shows who you are linked to with an Unlink button. Linking stores the in-game name and id the app reports, the address of your Overwolf avatar and a code made from your Overwolf username that cannot be turned back into it; the Privacy notes say so. Nothing about your matches is shown yet ([#321](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/321)).
- Once you have linked your account, the desktop app will be able to keep your in-game name and Overwolf avatar on the site up to date, so the header and Account page no longer go stale after you change either. It cannot move the link to a different game account (you link again for that), and does nothing if you have not linked. The new name and avatar replace the ones stored, and the call is added to the hour-long list kept to limit abuse; the Privacy notes say so. The app does not call this yet, so nothing changes on the site until its next release ([#346](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/346)).
- The desktop app will be able to show whether it is linked to your account (and as which in-game name), and to unlink itself, without opening the website. After it unlinks, the site header goes back to "Account" and the Account page shows no link, exactly as if you had pressed Unlink there; your website account and its data stay, and a link code you had asked for and not used stops working. The app never learns your email or website account id. The app does not call this yet, so nothing changes on the site until its next release ([#348](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/348)).
- The sidebar ad space can now show a Google ad once a Google ad unit is set up for it; no ad unit exists yet, so no Google ad appears and you still see the RivalsPulse "Coming soon" advert. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent message decides: a Google ad appears only if you chose Consent, and if you chose Do not consent, or have not chosen yet, you see the RivalsPulse advert. Elsewhere Google shows no message, and the Google ad appears a few seconds after the page loads. A new "Privacy and cookie settings" link in the footer, shown only where that message applies, lets you change your choice, and each new page you open gets a fresh ad. The Privacy notes explain the choice and how to change it. There is also a reusable affiliate-link disclosure line, not used anywhere yet ([#319](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/319)).
- Every page except the home page now has a sidebar with an ad space. At 1280px and wider it sits beside the page content and follows you as you scroll; on narrower screens it sits below the content. For now the space shows an ad for the RivalsPulse app that says "Coming soon" and links nowhere, because the app has no store listing yet. The space keeps the same size whatever fills it, so nothing on the page jumps, and it adds no ad code of its own ([#305](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/305)).
- You can sign in with Discord. A Sign in link in the header opens a sign-in page, and once you are signed in an Account link opens a page showing how you signed in, your email and when the account was created, with a Sign out button. Signing in sets a sign-in cookie (`HttpOnly`, plus a short-lived one while you sign in) and stores your Discord id, username, email (if Discord shares it) and the creation date, and Supabase also keeps your IP address in its sign-in log; the Privacy notes now say so and how to delete the account. Discord sign-in works on rivalspulse.com and localhost only ([#275](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/275)).
- You can delete your account yourself. The Account page has a "Delete your account" section: you choose what to remove, type `delete my account` and confirm; if you signed in more than ten minutes ago you are asked to sign in again first. It deletes your sign-in details and our row for you and signs you out; signing in with Discord again starts a new, empty account. "Remove account and forget me" is shown but switched off until you can link the desktop app ([#283](https://github.com/anthonyturner/rivals-pulse-web/pull/283)).
